## FDA does not approve most consumer products

FDA approval applies to specific product types such as new drugs and certain medical devices. FDA does not approve or certify ordinary consumer goods such as cups, bottles or kitchen tools, and it does not certify manufacturers. Saying such a product is "FDA approved" or "FDA certified" is misleading.

## Registration is not approval

A facility may be registered with FDA, but registration does not mean FDA has reviewed or approved the product. "FDA registered" should not be used to suggest approval.

## Food-contact materials

Materials that touch food are regulated by FDA rules for food-contact substances. For example, silicone rubber intended for repeated use with food is covered by 21 CFR 177.2600. A seller with a lab report showing the material meets that regulation can say the item is "made with food-grade silicone" and, if documented, that the material complies with the cited FDA regulation. Without a report, describe the material only.

## Safer wording

Instead of "FDA approved silicone cup", use "made with food-grade silicone" when the seller can support it, and ask the seller for the test report before mentioning any FDA regulation.
